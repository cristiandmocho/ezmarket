For this kind of product, the biggest mistake is turning it into:

> “just another supermarket search website”

The winning experience is usually:

* frictionless
* instantly useful
* financially valuable within seconds

Users should immediately feel:

> “This saves me money every single week.”

The second important thing:

Portugal’s supermarket ecosystem is chaotic:

* different promotions
* different units
* fake discounts
* loyalty-card prices
* private labels
* rotating campaigns

That chaos is your product opportunity.

The front-end should focus on:

1. saving money
2. reducing decision fatigue
3. helping plan shopping efficiently

not just showing product lists.

## Recommended product positioning

You are not building:

* a supermarket catalogue

You are building:

* a smart shopping assistant

Think:

* “Google Flights for groceries”
* “PCPartPicker for supermarket baskets”
* “Idealista for food prices”

# Key Features Worth Building

# 1. Basket Optimiser

This is your moat.

Algorithm:

```text
Goal:
Minimise total basket cost
```

Possible modes:

* single supermarket only
* minimise travel
* maximise savings
* preferred supermarkets
* avoid premium stores

This becomes very addictive.

---

# 2. “Fake Discount” Detection

Massive opportunity.

Example:

```text
“Was €9.99”
Actually:
€7.99 two weeks ago
```

Portuguese users will love this.

Could become viral.

---

# 3. Price History Graphs

Absolutely essential.

Users LOVE:

* historical lows
* trends
* timing purchases

Especially for:

* olive oil
* coffee
* meat
* detergents
* baby products

---

# 4. Smart Alternatives

Example:

```text
You selected:
Coca-Cola €2.49

Equivalent alternatives:
- Pepsi €1.89
- Private label cola €0.99
```

Very valuable.

---

# 5. Barcode Scanner

Extremely important for mobile.

User scans product in-store:

* instantly compare nearby prices

This makes your app practical.

---

# 6. Pantry Mode

Advanced but potentially huge.

Users track:

* recurring purchases
* consumption cycles

Then:

```text
You're likely running low on rice
```

Very sticky feature.

---

# 7. AI Shopping List Parser

Very useful.

User writes:

```text

```

AI converts into structured basket.

High perceived intelligence.

# UX recommendations

# Prioritise speed

This type of product lives or dies on:

* instant responses
* frictionless interaction

Avoid:

* heavy animations
* overloaded cards
* infinite ads
* supermarket-style clutter

---

# Mobile-first is mandatory

Most grocery decisions happen:

* in-store
* on the sofa
* during meal planning

Desktop is secondary.

---

# Optimise for repeated use

Your product should become:

* weekly habit
* not occasional utility

That means:

* saved baskets
* recurring alerts
* personalised insights

# Final recommendation

Your strongest differentiator is probably NOT:

* individual product comparison

It is:

> full basket optimisation + historical price intelligence

That combination creates:

* real savings
* habit formation
* strong retention
* shareability
* SEO traffic
* data moat

Most existing supermarket comparison tools fail because they only answer:

> “Where is this product cheaper?”

The much more valuable question is:

> “How do I spend the least possible money on my actual weekly shopping?”
